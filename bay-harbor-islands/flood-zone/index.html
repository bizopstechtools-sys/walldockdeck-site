Bay Harbor Islands · Miami-Dade County

What flood zone is Bay Harbor Islands in, and what does it require?

BFE +1 ft (state code)no extra town freeboard found

Bay Harbor Islands amended its flood chapter in August 2025 (Ord. No. 1131) and uses a 49% substantial-improvement and damage trigger. It bans manufactured homes and recreational vehicles and requires nonconversion agreements for enclosures below the required elevation.

Source: Bay Harbor Islands Code of Ordinances Ch. 7½ Flood Damage Prevention, Secs. 7½-10, 7½-11 (Ord. No. 1131); FEMA Community Status Book, Florida (www.fema.gov/cis/FL.html, report 10/03/2026); 2023 Florida Building Code, Residential R322.2.1, R322.3.2; 44 CFR 59.1; FEMA Flood Map Service Center · Verified 2026-10

The detail

Substantial improvement / damage49 percent of market value, no cumulative period - Sec. 7½-10, applied to FBC by Sec. 7½-11(b)(1).
Nonconversion agreementsRequired for enclosures below the required elevation and recorded before the certificate of occupancy - Sec. 7½-11(b)(2).
Critical facilitiesFBC minimum or the 500-year flood elevation plus one foot, whichever is higher - Sec. 7½-11(b)(3).
Manufactured homes and RVsNot permitted - Sec. 7½-11(c) (Ord. No. 1131, 8-13-2025).
More trigger points$457,000 building value -> $223,930 trigger; $718,000 -> $351,820.
FEMA flood mapFEMA's Community Status Book (report dated 3 Oct 2026) lists Bay Harbor Islands as NFIP community 120637 with a current effective FIRM dated 11 Sep 2009. Zones are set parcel by parcel, so no single zone covers the whole city - check your own address.
NFIP historyFEMA record: first FIRM 29 Sep 1972; regular NFIP program since 29 Sep 1972; entered CRS 1 Oct 1994.
Flood insurance discountCRS class 9 - 5% off NFIP premiums for policies in the Special Flood Hazard Area (class in effect 1 Apr 2023).
State minimum2023 Florida Building Code, Residential R322.2.1 and R322.3.2 - lowest floor (A zones) or bottom of the lowest horizontal structural member (V and Coastal A zones) at BFE + 1 ft or the design flood elevation, whichever is higher.
Look up your zoneEnter the address in FEMA's Flood Map Service Center (msc.fema.gov), the official public source for FIRMs; an elevation certificate shows how your floor compares with the BFE.
