Broward County · Seawalls

What are the seawall requirements in Broward County?

5.0ft NAVD88 by 2050

Broward is the one county in South Florida with a single standard behind every city, and the number most people quote is the wrong one. The standard is 5 ft NAVD88 by 2050. The 4 ft figure you will hear is an allowance until 2035 that the county says a municipality MAY grant - not a floor you are entitled to. Three cities answer it three different ways, so the number that applies to you is your city's, not the county's.

Source: Broward County Code Sec. 39-404, Art. XXV, Resiliency Standards for Tidal Flood Protection; Broward County Land Use Plan Policy 2.21.7; Broward County "Build It High, Keep It Dry" developer guidance; City of Hollywood Sea Walls and Other Tidal Flood Barriers brochure · Verified 2026-10

The detail

The standard5 ft NAVD88 by 1 January 2050. That is the figure the county is driving every shoreline to.
The 2035 allowanceUntil 2035 a municipality may allow 4 ft NAVD88, but only if the barrier is designed and constructed to accommodate 5 ft NAVD88 by 1 January 2050.
It is not a rightThe county's own developer guidance says the allowance "may be granted by the municipality." It is your city's call. Budget for 5 ft and treat 4 ft as the exception you have to ask for.
Where it comes fromBroward County Code Section 39-404, Article XXV, Resiliency Standards for Tidal Flood Protection, and Broward County Land Use Plan Policy 2.21.7.
Every city had to adopt itLocal governments were required to adopt an implementing ordinance by 13 February 2022, so the standard reaches inside every municipality in the county.
HollywoodTook the allowance. Its own guidance requires 4 ft NAVD88 before 1 January 2035, then 5 ft by 2050.
Fort LauderdaleSet 5.0 ft NAVD88. An application before 1 January 2035 may build to 4.0 ft if the wall is designed to reach 5.0 ft by 2050.
Pompano Beach5 ft NAVD88 minimum and 5 ft 10 in maximum. The only city in the county that caps the height as well as setting a floor.
The practical pointBuild to 4 ft today and the wall has to come up again before 2050. Ask what it costs to build to 5 ft now, while the barge is already there.
