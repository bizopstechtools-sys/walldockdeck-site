Glossary · 22 terms

The words on a seawall permit, quote or notice

Plain-English definitions of the terms that turn up when you are dealing with a seawall, a dock or a code notice. Where a definition states a figure or what the law requires, it names the primary source and the date we checked it.

Term

Base flood elevation (BFE)

How high floodwater is expected to reach in the 1-percent-annual-chance flood.

Read it →Term

Benthic survey

A survey of what lives on the bottom where in-water work is planned.

Read it →Term

Datum

The zero point a height is measured from. A seawall height means nothing without one.

Read it →Term

Deadman anchor

A buried block behind the wall that a tie-back rod pulls against.

Read it →Term

Environmental Resource Permit (ERP)

Florida's permit for work in, on or over wetlands and other surface waters.

Read it →Term

Filter fabric

Geotextile behind the wall that lets water through and keeps soil in.

Read it →Term

Flood Insurance Rate Map (FIRM)

FEMA's official flood map: where your flood zone and BFE come from.

Read it →Term

King tide

An everyday name for exceptionally high tides, not a scientific term.

Read it →Term

Living shoreline

A shoreline stabilised with natural materials such as plants, sand or rock.

Read it →Term

NAVD88

The vertical datum most South Florida cities now use to state a seawall height.

Read it →Term

NGVD29

The older vertical datum still found on old surveys, permits and flood maps.

Read it →Term

Notice of Commencement

The form an owner records and posts before work starts, under s. 713.13.

Read it →Term

Notice to Owner

A subcontractor's or supplier's notice that protects their right to a lien.

Read it →Term

Release of lien

A signed waiver of lien rights for work already paid for, on the statutory form.

Read it →Term

Riprap

Loose rock at the base of a seawall that absorbs wave energy and protects the toe.

Read it →Term

Seawall cap

The concrete beam along the top of a seawall, and usually the part a height rule measures.

Read it →Term

Sheet pile

Interlocking panels driven into the ground to form the wall itself.

Read it →Term

Sovereign submerged lands

State-owned land under navigable water, which needs state consent to build on.

Read it →Term

Substantial improvement and substantial repair

The threshold that pulls the whole wall, not just the repair, up to the current rule.

Read it →Term

Tidal flood barrier

The broader term Broward codes use: a seawall, or anything else doing the same job.

Read it →Term

Tie-back anchor

A rod or cable from the back of the wall into the ground, holding the wall upright.

Read it →Term

Weep hole

A drainage opening low in the wall that lets trapped water out as the tide falls.
