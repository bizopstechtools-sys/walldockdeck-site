Key Largo · Monroe County

How high does a seawall have to be in Key Largo?

No minimumset in local code

Key Largo is unincorporated, so Monroe County's Land Development Code governs, and it sets no minimum or maximum seawall top elevation in NAVD88, NGVD29 or any other datum. The only height rule is relative: your yard behind the wall must sit at least six inches below the top of the wall. The design elevation comes from your engineer and your state and federal permits, not from a county number.

Source: Monroe County Land Development Code Sec. 118-12(k) (Shoreline Setback - Bulkheads, seawalls, and riprap) and Sec. 122-31(d)(1) (Floodplain Management - Other development); LDC online version May 18, 2026 · Verified 2026-10

The detail

No fixed elevationMonroe County LDC Sec. 118-12(k), Bulkheads, seawalls, and riprap, sets where and when seawalls are allowed but states no minimum or maximum top elevation in any datum.
The one height ruleExisting grade landward of the bulkhead shall be at least six inches lower than the top of the bulkhead. Sec. 118-12(k)(2). This is measured against your own yard, not a fixed elevation.
Cap widthA seawall without a principal use may have a cap no more than two feet wide, and docking attachments such as davits, cleats and platforms are allowed only as accessory to a principal use. Sec. 118-12(k)(1).
Where a vertical wall is allowedOnly to stabilize a severely eroding shoreline on a manmade canal, channel or basin, and only if native vegetation or riprap with filter cloth is not feasible. No new seawalls, bulkheads or other hardened vertical structures on open water. Sec. 118-12(k)(3).
Repair and replacementA lawfully existing, deteriorated seawall may be repaired or replaced and is exempt from the nonsubstantial-improvement limits except on known or potential sea turtle nesting beaches. Keep the existing footprint to the maximum extent practicable. Sec. 118-12(k)(4).
Turtle beachesNo seawalls, bulkheads or riprap on or waterward of a beach berm that is known or potential marine turtle nesting area. Sec. 118-12(k)(7).
V zonesIn coastal high hazard areas (Zone V), bulkheads and seawalls are allowed only if also authorized by the appropriate federal, state or local authority, located outside and not attached to buildings, and backed by a registered design professional's analysis showing no harmful diversion of floodwaters or wave runup and reflection. Sec. 122-31(d)(1).
