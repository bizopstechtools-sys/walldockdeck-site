What are the seawall requirements in Miami-Dade County?

No county height rulethe number is set by your city

Miami-Dade works the opposite way to Broward. The county runs the environmental permit through Chapter 24, but it sets no countywide seawall elevation — that is your city's decision, and the cities differ sharply. What the county does control is how far waterward you may rebuild, and that single measurement decides whether your permit takes weeks or months.

Source: Miami-Dade County Code Sec. 24-48.2 (as amended 2024); City of Miami Beach Code Sec. 54-62(a), Ordinance 2025-4754 · Verified 2026-09

The detail

The county's partChapter 24 environmental permitting through DERM, which applies inside every city in the county.
The 18-inch ruleA seawall rebuilt up to 18 inches waterward of its existing line qualifies for an administrative short-form permit. Beyond that it goes to a public hearing before the Board of County Commissioners.
Why that mattersIt is the difference between a permit measured in weeks and one measured in months. The threshold was raised from 12 inches in 2024, so older guidance is out of date.
Where it is writtenMiami-Dade County Code Section 24-48.2.
Elevation is municipalThe county sets no height. Miami Beach requires 5.7 ft NAVD88. Other cities set their own, and several have not published one at all.
Miami Beach specifically5.7 ft NAVD88 for new or substantially improved walls, with an interim 4.0 ft allowed for lesser repairs if the wall is built to accept 5.7 ft later. Substantial now means repairs of $500 or more per linear foot, raised from $300 in July 2025.
Budget for a bondSome cities in the county require a refundable bond on top of the contract. Confirm the current amount with the city - it is not in the contractor's bid. Ask before you sign.
