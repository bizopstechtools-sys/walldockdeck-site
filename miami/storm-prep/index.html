What are the storm and debris rules in Miami?

Evacuate earlycity marinas recommend leaving before a watch

Miami's own marinas say city mooring facilities are not safe in tropical storms or hurricanes and recommend vessels leave before a hurricane watch or warning; if an owner does not, the Harbormaster may move or secure the boat and charge a fee. Licensees must keep a hurricane plan on file. Private seawalls must be kept in good repair, with 180 days to start abating after a citation.

Source: City of Miami Code of Ordinances Secs. 29-95, 50-15, 50-16, 50-17 (Municode); s. 327.59, Fla. Stat. (2026) · Verified 2026-10

The detail

City mooring facilitiesCity mooring facilities are not safe locations for vessels during tropical storms or hurricanes; the city recommends vessels evacuate prior to a hurricane watch or warning. If a vessel is not removed before a storm, the city may remove it if reasonable or have the Harbormaster secure it, may charge a reasonable fee, and is not liable for storm damage - Code Sec. 50-16
Hurricane plan on fileLicensees at all city marinas and mooring facilities must file and maintain a plan with emergency contacts and the names of people authorized to secure the vessel in advance of a tropical storm or hurricane - Sec. 50-17
Emergency vessel repairsLicensees consent to the dockmaster or harbormaster making emergency repairs (failed bilge, fuel or sewage pumps, leaks, chafed or broken lines); costs are billed to the owner, payable within 24 hours of return - Sec. 50-15
Seawall repair clockOwners must keep seawalls and other shoreline protection in good repair; privately owned waterfront improvements are privately maintained even where they extend into public land. On citation: begin abating within 180 days by retaining a licensed contractor to apply for a permit, begin repairs within 18 months and finish within 24 months (12 and 18 months for walls designed to be raised); extensions in six-month increments - Sec. 29-95
State lawPrivate marinas may not require vessels to leave after a hurricane watch or warning, but may by contract remove or secure a vessel that is not promptly removed after a tropical storm or hurricane watch, for a reasonable fee - s. 327.59(1)-(3), Fla. Stat. (2026)
