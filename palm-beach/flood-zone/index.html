Palm Beach · Palm Beach County

What flood zone is Palm Beach in, and what does it require?

BFE +1 ft (state code)no extra town freeboard found

The Town of Palm Beach counts improvements over one year: work in a one-year period reaching 50% of market value is a substantial improvement, with the clock running from the first permit issued after 9 October 2021. No extra freeboard number appears in Chapter 50.

Source: Town of Palm Beach Code of Ordinances Ch. 50 Floods, Secs. 50-137.5, 50-141; FEMA Community Status Book, Florida (www.fema.gov/cis/FL.html, report 10/03/2026); 2023 Florida Building Code, Residential R322.2.1, R322.3.2; 44 CFR 59.1; FEMA Flood Map Service Center · Verified 2026-10

The detail

Substantial improvementOne-year period, cumulative cost of 50% or more; the period begins on the date of the first permit issued after October 9, 2021 - Sec. 50-137.5, applied to FBC by Sec. 50-141 (Ord. No. 030-2024).
Substantial damage50 percent of market value - Sec. 50-137.5.
Accessory structuresNon-elevated accessory structures use flood damage-resistant materials below BFE + 1 ft and keep mechanical, plumbing and electrical systems at or above BFE + 1 ft - Sec. 50-140 (Ord. No. 010-2022).
Subdivisions in AH and AO zonesDrainage paths must guide floodwaters around and away from proposed structures - Sec. 50-145 (Ord. No. 16-2017).
FEMA flood mapFEMA's Community Status Book (report dated 3 Oct 2026) lists Palm Beach as NFIP community 120220 with a current effective FIRM dated 20 Dec 2024. Zones are set parcel by parcel, so no single zone covers the whole city - check your own address.
NFIP historyFEMA record: first flood hazard boundary map 13 Sep 1974; first FIRM 15 May 1978; regular NFIP program since 15 May 1978; entered CRS 1 Oct 1992.
Flood insurance discountCRS class 6 - 20% off NFIP premiums for policies in the Special Flood Hazard Area (class in effect 1 Oct 2020).
State minimum2023 Florida Building Code, Residential R322.2.1 and R322.3.2 - lowest floor (A zones) or bottom of the lowest horizontal structural member (V and Coastal A zones) at BFE + 1 ft or the design flood elevation, whichever is higher.
Look up your zoneEnter the address in FEMA's Flood Map Service Center (msc.fema.gov), the official public source for FIRMs; an elevation certificate shows how your floor compares with the BFE.
